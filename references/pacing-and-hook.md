# Pacing, tightening and the hook

## Edit from the transcript, not the timeline

1. Word-level transcript (stable-ts / whisper is installed). Correct names and
   numbers by ear. ASR word timings are approximate: zero-length words and
   display edits ("40" to "$40") need reconciliation before they drive motion.
2. Mark the material: HOOK candidates (the most specific, surprising, costly or
   contradictory lines; numbers), BEATS (each distinct point), TURN (opinion,
   twist), END (verdict plus a human moment).
3. Reorder. The recording order is a first draft. Cold-open on the hook even if
   it was said at minute two, then stakes, then beats in rising order.
4. Write a beat sheet, one row per line: source in/out, words, shot, device or
   graphic, caption treatment, SFX, note. The EDL and every graphic cue are
   generated from it. Key cues to words, not to typed seconds, so a re-cut
   moves them automatically.

## Tightening

- Remove fillers ("you know", "obviously", "whatever", "yeah so", filler
  "like", um/uh), false starts, repeats, restated sentences (keep the better
  take) and throat clears. Check each hit; "like" and "so" are often real words.
- Qualifiers ("I guess", "kind of", "I think", "about", "seems") are not filler
  when they carry his honest uncertainty. Remove one only if the claim stays
  exactly as true without it; otherwise keep it and keep it in the captions.
- Air inside a sentence: 2-3 frames. Between sentences: 4-6 frames. Before a
  punchline or after a question to the viewer: 8-12 frames, deliberately.
- Cut at the energy minimum between words (search +/-40 ms), never mid-word.
  10-20 ms audio crossfade on every cut; continuous room tone underneath, never
  digital silence.
- Never time-stretch speech.
- Target 160-190 wpm for info content. Adem's raw delivery is ~135 wpm with
  many thinking pauses, so expect a 20-30% shorter cut.
- Length follows content, but every 30 s must earn its place. Long videos (he
  has approved 3-4 minutes) need a re-hook every 30-45 s: a new question, a
  bigger number, "but here's the weird part".

## The hook (first 3 seconds)

Frame 1 is a thumbnail and an argument:
- the face at C or X, or the hero visual full-frame; never a slow wide;
- 3-7 words of hook text readable in frame 1 for muted autoplay: a claim, not a
  title ("$5 lasted five minutes.");
- motion already happening (push in progress, type mid-arrival);
- the first spoken words are the hook line. No greeting, no "so", no "in this
  video".
By 1.5 s a second visual event (cut, number slam, cutaway). By 3 s the viewer
knows the topic and the tension.

Hook shapes that fit a first-hand tester:
- the receipt: "I paid $X for Y. Here's what I got."
- the escalation: two receipts, the second worse ($5 to 5 min, then $40 to 14 min).
- the contradiction: "They sell always-on agents. Then meter them by the minute."
- the warning: "Don't top up until you've seen this."
The hook compresses a true line he said. Never invent the fact behind it.

## Retention structure

hook (0-3 s) -> stakes (3-10 s: why the viewer should care) -> beats (claim ->
proof visual -> his reaction) -> re-hooks -> turn or opinion -> close (verdict
line plus a human moment) -> end on a cut or a beat, not a slow fade. If
there's a call to action, make it a question for the comments.

## Rhythm is contrast

Three or four fast cuts, then a 3-5 s hold on something sincere or complex.
Uniform speed is as dull as uniform slowness. Use black beats (6-10 frames)
before big reveals. If there is music, land section changes on downbeats.
